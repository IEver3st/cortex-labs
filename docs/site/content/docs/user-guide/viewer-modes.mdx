title: Viewer Modes
description: Choose the right preview mode for your asset type and workflow.
---

Cortex Studio’s viewer supports multiple modes, optimized for different workflows.

## Vehicle mode (Alt + 1)

Everything for a vehicle in one place: liveries, paint, mod kits and texture checks. Projects, recent tabs and saved vehicles from the earlier Livery and All modes open here.

- **Livery** (step 2) goes on the paint material Cortex detects, shown under **Apply to** as **Detected:** and the material's name, so your texture lands where the game puts a livery without picking a mesh. There it is clamped to the livery sheet and uses the model's livery UV set. If no paint material is found, it covers every mesh. **Apply to** can also put it on **All meshes**, which is the way to check a blank template's UV layout and spot unexpected UV islands, or on one material or mesh.
- **Glass** (step 3) adds window decals or tint on the glass Cortex detects. **Apply to** points it at **All meshes**, one material or mesh, or **None** instead.
- **Model's own textures** (under step 2, desktop app) loads the vehicle's GTA textures, so the rest of the vehicle looks as it does in game around your livery. It is a best-effort preview, not GTA shader parity. Turn it off for plain paint.
- Optional **Exterior Only** behavior to avoid pushing livery textures onto interior/glass candidates.
- The inspector has five tabs: **Paint**, **Liveries**, **Mods**, **Scene** and **Meta**.
- The **Paint** tab holds the vehicle's six game colours, as carcols sets them: **Primary**, **Secondary**, **Pearlescent**, **Wheels**, **Interior trim** and **Dashboard**. Each mesh takes the colour its shader's paint layer names (`matDiffuseColor` `2, layer, 0`), so lights, glass, tires and unpainted parts keep their own look. Pick a colour from the game palette, grouped by finish (Metallic, Matte, Util, Worn and Metals), or type any hex for a custom colour. Primary and Secondary also take a finish; a palette colour brings its own, and changing it makes a custom colour. Pearlescent shows as a sheen on glossy primary paint. Slots the loaded model never uses say **Not on this model**. The Meta tab's colour presets fill all six slots.
- The **Showroom** stage (Scene tab) parks the vehicle in a dark studio under an aimable spotlight, on a floor you choose: **Concrete**, **Polished**, **Tiles**, **Marble**, **Checker**, **Epoxy**, **Asphalt**, **Wood** or **Tread plate**. Sirens shares the same floor picker.
- **Turntable** (Scene tab) spins the vehicle in place under the lights at a speed you set, in Vehicle, Sirens and Multi. Turned off, the vehicle settles back facing forward. Captures from a named shot (Front, Side, 3/4 and so on) always show the vehicle at rest.
- **Capture** (also in EUP) sits folded to one line at the bottom of the dock; click it to open. It saves one PNG per chosen angle, named `<model>_<angle>.png`, into a `<model>` folder inside your preview export folder. A preview folder outside your user folders (another drive, for example) takes the shots directly, and the result says so. Cortex asks before replacing shots that already exist, and the result counts only the files it wrote, naming any angle that couldn't be captured and the error that stopped a run. **Save this view** saves the viewfinder as framed; in the browser it downloads the PNG, and capturing angles needs the desktop app.

### A folder of vehicles

**Open a folder of vehicles**, under the model on the Model step (desktop app), shows every vehicle in a folder on one screen, so you can go through a pack one car at a time instead of opening each model.

- Pick one resource or a folder of resources, such as a `[cars]` category. With more than one resource, the vehicles are grouped under each resource's name. Picking a folder inside a resource, such as its `stream` folder, lists the vehicles that resource's `vehicles.meta` names in that folder.
- In a resource with a `vehicles.meta`, the models it names are the vehicles, so mod kit parts kept in the same folder as the car stay out of the list. Without one, or when it names no vehicles, every `.yft` is listed except the parts its `carcols.meta` kits use. A vehicle opens its `_hi` model when it has one.

#### Pack and Vehicle

With a folder open, **Pack** and **Vehicle** in the title bar switch between the whole folder and the vehicle you're working on.

- **Pack** names the pack and shows each vehicle as a snapshot, with its model name and display name or file. The open vehicle is shown in opposite ink. A vehicle with liveries staged on the **Liveries** tab and not yet written says how many, and a warning sign marks a vehicle whose meta has errors the **Meta** tab explains, such as a livery list without `FLAG_HAS_LIVERY`. Click a vehicle to open it in **Vehicle**.
- **Vehicle** is the usual workspace with the folder at the top of the dock: the open vehicle's place in it (`08 / 18`), and arrows that open the previous or next vehicle. **Alt + PageUp** and **Alt + PageDown** do the same from either view. Clicking the pack's name goes back to **Pack**.
- **Filter vehicles** narrows the pack by model name, display name or resource, and the arrows and shortcuts step through what it leaves. **Esc** in the filter clears it.
- **Missing models** lists `vehicles.meta` entries the folder holds no model for, and **Couldn't read** lists folders or meta files that were skipped. A very large folder is listed only in part, and the pack says so. A folder inside a resource doesn't report the vehicles it leaves out as missing.
- The buttons beside the folder's path list it again, such as after you add or remove vehicles, open another folder, or close the folder.
- Liveries you add on the **Liveries** tab but haven't written stay with their vehicle while you move through the folder.

#### Snapshots

A snapshot is taken from the same 3/4 camera shot for every vehicle, the first time each session the vehicle is open with its textures loaded. Snapshots are kept in Cortex's cache, not beside your files, so the pack shows them again next time without loading any vehicle. Until a vehicle has one, its tile shows its name, less any prefix every vehicle in the pack shares.

**Take snapshots** opens each vehicle that has none in turn, behind the pack, and takes one, then reopens the vehicle you had open. It counts its way through them; **Stop** ends it early, and so does opening a vehicle yourself. A vehicle that doesn't load, or hasn't loaded after two minutes, is skipped and counted at the end. Once every vehicle has one, the button reads **Retake snapshots** and takes them all again, such as after you write liveries or turn the model's own textures on or off; a snapshot shows the vehicle the way the viewer showed it. Because each vehicle opens as if you had clicked it, a vehicle's first load converts its model, so a large pack takes a while the first time.

- The folder and the view you were on are saved with the workspace. Cortex can read a folder you open until the app closes; after a restart, a folder outside your user folder lists again only when it is the open vehicle's own resource. Otherwise the pack says it couldn't read the folder, and **Open folder…** asks for it again.
- Focus mode hides the folder bar with the dock.

### Liveries

The **Liveries** tab (desktop app) switches between the liveries the vehicle has in game and writes a whole set of your images into the vehicle at once.

- **Livery**, at the top of the tab, switches between the liveries the vehicle has in game. It lists the texture liveries in the vehicle's texture dictionary chain (`<name>_sign_1`, `_sign_2` and so on, what `SetVehicleLivery` picks between) and the mod kit's livery mods (`VMT_LIVERY_MOD`). The arrows beside it step to the previous or next livery. Texture liveries use the kit's `<liveryNames>` text when the resource registers it, otherwise they show as Livery 1, Livery 2 and so on. **As modelled** shows the textures the model binds. A livery mod's model is only a placeholder, so its livery texture is shown on the paint instead; it covers a texture livery in game, so picking one clears the other. Liveries need **Model's own textures**, and texture liveries show even when the resource has no mod kit. A texture you loaded in step 2 stays on the meshes it's applied to, so a game livery only shows on the rest (with **All meshes**, nowhere until you unload it); the Livery section says so while one is loaded.

#### Writing liveries

The list under **In game** shows the vehicle's texture liveries as the game numbers them.

- **Names** is the texture name, with `#` where the livery number goes. It starts as the `<name>_sign_#` the paint uses (for example `fpiu_sign_#`), else the one the vehicle's texture dictionaries hold, else one made from the model name.
- Each row is one livery: its number, a thumbnail and its texture name. Liveries already in the vehicle say **In the vehicle** with their largest size. Click a row to preview it on the Livery step.
- **Add liveries** picks PNG, JPG, WebP, BMP, TIFF, PSD, PDN or DDS images, or drop them on the tab. New images follow the vehicle's liveries in file-name order (`livery2` before `livery10`), up to livery 99. Dropping images on a row replaces that livery with the first of them; the rest take the next free numbers. The **X** on a row takes an image back out, and **Clear** takes them all. The images you add are kept with the workspace until you write them.
- The bin on a livery already in the vehicle marks it for removal, for when you have fewer liveries than the vehicle came with: its name is struck through and it says **Removed on write**, and the undo arrow keeps it after all. Nothing is removed until you write, and editing **Names** clears the marks. An image dropped on a marked livery replaces it instead, and new images follow the liveries that stay, so marking the last livery and adding an image replaces it. Removing one from the middle leaves a gap, which **Checks** points out. A dictionary can't be left without any textures.
- When the Livery step shows an image that isn't one of these liveries, **Add `<file>` as livery N** adds it.
- **Write N liveries…** (or **Write N, remove N…**, or **Remove N liveries…**) shows what changes before anything is written: each texture dictionary it writes to, how many textures it adds, replaces or removes there, and at what size. A removed livery leaves every dictionary that holds it. Your images go into every dictionary beside the model that already holds the liveries (`<vehicle>+hi.ytd` and `<vehicle>.ytd` often both do), scaled to the size and mip count that dictionary's liveries use. A vehicle without liveries gets them in its `<vehicle>.ytd`, at the image's size rounded to a power of two between 64 and 4096. Images are stored as DXT5; a DDS already at the right size goes in unchanged. **Checks** point out images that are scaled up or stretched, gaps in the numbers (the game counts liveries 1, 2, 3… in order), and names the paint doesn't use.
- Each `.ytd` is first copied to a `.bak` named with the date and time, and the write is all or nothing: **Stop**, or any failure, leaves every file as it was. Afterwards the model reloads with the changed liveries; restart the resource in game to load them.
- Only the vehicle's own texture dictionaries change, and only with your images. Editing a vehicle you bought or downloaded follows its licence, the same as editing its files by hand; escrow-encrypted dictionaries can't be read, so they can't be written. Only legacy GTA V dictionaries can be written, and a vehicle needs a `.ytd` of its own beside the model.

### Mod kits

The **Mods** inspector tab previews a vehicle's mod kit parts on the loaded `.yft` (desktop app only).

- Cortex reads the resource's `carcols.meta` and `carvariations.meta` and lists the kit linked to the model under `<kits>`. A kit that only names the model, or the only kit in a single-vehicle resource, is shown with a warning, because the game will not offer it until carvariations links it.
- The game livery, including the kit's livery mods, is picked in the **Liveries** tab (see [Liveries](#liveries)).
- Each slot (spoiler, front bumper and so on) lists its visible mods in carcols order. Pick one per slot, or **Stock**. Linked models load with their mod.
- Switching a part animates on the model: the old part drops away and the new one lands from above. Extras switched in the Meta tab or in Sirens do the same. Parts the model loads with, Kit Shoot captures, and **Reduce motion** (in Settings or Windows) switch at once.
- Slots use the kit's carcols `<slotNames>` when it names them. Parts use the text the resource registers for their `modShopLabel` with `AddTextEntry` or `AddTextEntryByHash` in its Lua or JavaScript files; without one, the label itself is shown. Escrow-encrypted scripts can't be read, so their names don't appear.
- Mod `.yft` files are found anywhere in the resource, including `stream/` subfolders. A `_hi` version is used when present.
- Turn on **Model's own textures** to see textured parts. A mod uses textures it embeds first, then the vehicle's texture dictionary chain, as in game. Textures that are in neither are reported as not found.
- Stock meshes bound to a bone in the selected mod's `turnOffBones` are hidden. Meshes that span several bones stay visible.
- A part follows the bone its carcols entry names in `<bone>` (a linked model uses its `<linkMods>` entry when the kit has one). Door badges on `door_dside_f` open with the driver door, and parts on `boot` lift with the boot, as in game. Parts on `chassis` stay put even when they sit on a door or the boot, because the game doesn't move them either. Clicking a part on a door opens that door.
- The parts and livery you pick are saved with the workspace and load again when you reopen it from Home or restart the app. To keep a setup you can reopen later in a new tab, use **Save** on the **Saved vehicle** row under the model (see [Saved vehicles](/docs/user-guide/workspaces#saved-vehicles)).
- Sirens has the same **Mods** tab, with the **Livery** switcher at its top and without Kit shoot.

#### Builds

Type a name under **Builds** and press **Save** to keep the parts picked above as a build for this vehicle model. **Apply** picks those parts again. A build that names a slot or part the kit no longer has leaves that slot stock.

#### Kit shoot

**Kit shoot** photographs the kit and writes the results to a new folder inside your preview export folder: `<model>/kit-shoot-<kit>-<date>-<time>/`. Your picks, paint and grid come back when the run ends. Picks are locked while it runs, and **Cancel** stops it after the current step; files already written stay, and the report marks the run as partial.

- **Every part** shoots each part of the selected slots, starting each slot with its base: stock, or the part from your current picks when **Parts go on** is **Current build**. Other slots keep their base, so only one thing changes per shot.
- **Camera: Best angle** first renders the slot's base and every part from 17 directions around and above the vehicle (plus a cabin camera for interior slots), keeps the direction where the most parts visibly change, and crops to the area they change. The shots are then rendered at full resolution through that crop, so they are real close-ups, and every part in a slot uses the same camera. Engine bay slots hide the stock `bonnet` and the trunk slot hides the stock `boot` so the parts can be seen. **This view** shoots everything from the viewfinder as framed when the run starts.
- **Builds** shoots the chosen builds (and **Current build**) from each chosen angle. **Extra paints** takes GTA colour indices, such as `0, 27, 64`, and shoots each build again with that primary colour.
- **Before and after** pairs every part with its slot's base (in Builds, every build with the stock vehicle at the same angle and paint). **Side by side** places the frames next to each other; **Wipe** divides one frame through the change. **Zoom to the change** crops both frames to the area that changed.
- **Sheet per slot** writes one labelled grid per slot (or one grid of builds). **Cycle video** records the parts (or builds) in turn with their names. Videos record in real time.
- Shots render at the chosen format (4:5, 1:1, 16:9 or 9:16) independent of the window size. **Double size** doubles them, up to 4096 pixels on the long edge. Your watermark from Settings is applied to every image.

While a run goes, the panel shows each shot as it is written. When it ends, the **review** opens over the workspace: slots (or builds) on the left, the selected shot large, and a filmstrip below. **Compare** lays the shot over its base (stock, the build's part, or stock at the same angle and paint) with a handle you drag, or move with the arrow keys once it has focus. **Saved pair**, **Sheet** and **Video** show the files the run wrote. **Checks** lists every finding by type, and **View** jumps to the part. Use the left and right arrow keys for shots, up and down for slots, `C` to toggle Compare, and `Esc` to close. **Review shots** on the result card reopens it, and **Open an earlier shoot** opens any run from its `kit-shoot.json`.

Every run writes `kit-shoot.json`, described in [Kit shoot manifest](/docs/reference/kit-shoot-manifest), `report.html`, which shows every image and lists the checks below, and a small JPEG preview of each shot under `thumbs/`.

| Check | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `MODEL_MISSING` | A part's model or linked model isn't in the resource. |
| `LOAD_FAILED` | A part's model or textures failed to load, or were still loading after 90 seconds. |
| `TEXTURES_FAILED`, `TEXTURES_MISSING` | Some of a part's textures couldn't load or weren't found. |
| `NO_VISIBLE_CHANGE` | The part looks the same as the slot's base from its camera. |
| `DUPLICATE_PART` | Two parts in a slot look identical. |
| `STOCK_NOT_HIDDEN` | A note: the part lists `turnOffBones`, but no stock mesh uses them. Packs often list bones that only other mods use. |
| `UNNAMED_PART` | The part's `modShopLabel` has no `AddTextEntry`. Only checked when the resource registers some names. |
| `KIT_NOT_LINKED` | carvariations.meta doesn't list the kit. |
| `BUILD_PARTS_DROPPED` | A saved build names parts the kit doesn't have. |
| `CAPTURE_FAILED` | A file couldn't be rendered or written. |

Shots show the Studio preview, not the game renderer, so materials are a best-effort match. Turn on **Model's own textures** first, or parts are shot untextured and the report notes it. Visual checks such as `NO_VISIBLE_CHANGE` depend on the camera.

## EUP mode (Alt + 3)

Best for **clothing models**.

- Intended for `.ydd` workflows.
- Uses clothing-friendly model scanning settings.
- Clothing stands upright and faces the **Front** shot, converted from GTA's Z-up axes. If an item still faces the wrong way, the **X**, **Y** and **Z** buttons on the bottom bar turn it 90 degrees at a time and stand it back on the floor; the reset button next to them undoes the turns. The turns are saved with the workspace.
- Picking a clothing model also loads its own texture dictionary into the **Uniform** slot: the `_diff_` `.ytd` beside it (`<pack>^task_diff_000_a_uni.ytd` for `<pack>^task_000_u.ydd`). A texture you chose yourself stays. Turn this off with **Load EUP Textures** in Settings > Viewer. You can also pick a `.ytd` as the uniform texture; it shows the dictionary's diffuse texture and reloads when you save it.
- The **Workbench / Showroom** switch on the viewfinder works here too. The showroom stands the clothing in a dark studio under one spotlight, with the floor, light direction, spot strength and reflections in the Scene tab, and **Turntable** spins it. New EUP tabs start from the EUP scene in Settings > Studio, the one EUP Studio uses, including its rim light.
- **Surface** in the Material tab sets how the clothing takes light: the type (Paint, Chrome, Plastic, Metal, Glass), **Light response**, **Gloss**, **Roughness** and **Clearcoat**.

Use this mode when:

- You’re previewing Emergency Uniform Pack assets.

## Multi mode (Alt + 4)

Best for **comparing vehicles**.

- Lines up to six vehicles (`.yft`, `.clmesh` or `.dff`) in one scene. Each has its own model, livery, window design and paint (the same six game colours as Livery).
- In the desktop app a `.yft` loads through the CodeWalker bridge, as in Vehicle: wheels and the vehicle's own game textures, tinted by its paint. A livery replaces the textures on the meshes it covers. Vehicles load one at a time in lineup order; the ones still waiting say so in their Model step. If the bridge can't read a model, the vehicle shows its shape only and the Model step says why.
- **Add** creates an empty slot, the duplicate button copies the selected vehicle (same model, paint and liveries) so you can try another livery on it, and the arrows reorder the lineup. The eye button hides a vehicle without unloading it.
- **Use on all** puts the selected livery on every vehicle; **Use this paint on every vehicle** does the same for paint.
- Click a vehicle in the viewfinder to select it; the gizmo follows the selection. **Move** on the deck (Alt + G) drags the vehicle across the floor, and **Rotate** swaps it for a ring that turns the vehicle in place. Click the lit button again to hide the gizmo. These buttons stay on the deck even when camera controls are in the panel. **Line up** on the deck parks every visible vehicle side by side in lineup order and keeps each vehicle's facing.
- Dropping several models on the viewfinder fills the selected empty slot and adds the rest. Dropping an image sets the selected vehicle's livery.
- Liveries and window designs reload when you save them, on every vehicle that uses the file.
- **Save this view**, under Capture at the bottom of the lineup panel, saves a PNG of the viewfinder exactly as framed, without the move or rotate gizmo, to your preview folder (you pick one the first time) as `lineup_photo_<date-time>.png`, with your watermark when one is set.
- The **Showroom** stage parks the lineup in a dark studio. With **Spotlights** set to **Each vehicle**, every visible vehicle gets its own spotlight, floor pool, beam and contact shadow, all from the **Light from** direction, and they follow a vehicle you move. **Whole lineup** uses one wide light instead. The lights hang high above the lineup so they stay out of frame. With the **Turntable** on, each vehicle spins in its own bay. The Scene tab also picks the floor: **Concrete**, **Polished**, **Tiles**, **Marble**, **Checker**, **Epoxy**, **Asphalt**, **Wood** or **Tread plate**.

Clothing is not compared here. Use **EUP Studio** and add each piece under **Addon pieces** to wear several at once. Multi tabs saved with uniforms open empty and say where the clothing went.

Use this mode when:

- You want to compare liveries across a fleet, or several liveries on the same vehicle.
- You’re checking scale, paint or glass consistency across models.

## Sirens mode (Alt + 9)

Best for **emergency lights**. The vehicle loads the way All loads it, whole and with its own textures, and the workspace is built around its carcols siren setting: a step grid under the viewfinder, the selected sirens and the setting in the inspector, and the regular lamps, extras and stage beside them. Workbench and Showroom both work. See [Sirens](/docs/user-guide/sirens).
