---
title: Sirens
description: A workspace for a vehicle's emergency lights. Edit its carcols.meta siren setting or its ELS VCF while it runs on the model, then save it back.
---

Sirens is its own workspace (**Alt + 9**, or **Sirens** on Home). You can [build a pattern without a vehicle](#patterns-without-a-vehicle), or open a `.yft` vehicle and the workspace reads the carcols siren setting that carvariations `sirenSettings` links to it, starts the pattern, and opens the setting for editing. Every edit shows on the model as you make it. Nothing is written to disk until you save. Vehicles that use ELS switch to their VCF instead; see [ELS](#els).

The vehicle loads the way it does in Vehicle: the whole model, with its own GTA textures (turn **Model's own textures** off under the vehicle for plain paint). Exterior only does not apply here. The **Workbench** and **Showroom** stages both work, and Night darkens either one.

## Layout

- **Sequencer.** The left and centre of the window, the full height, laid out like Siren Tool. A rail about a fifth of the window wide runs down its left side. It opens with the setting's name and id, then rows for **File** (the carcols.meta it came from, when it has one; its icon opens the file in your editor), **Status** (whether the model matches the file) and **Checks**, which stays folded to one row that counts the most serious kind (blocking, warnings or notes) in its colour; click it for the list. The refresh button beside the name reads the meta files again, and on a vehicle the **Carcols sirens** and **ELS** switch sits under the rows (in the centre while the vehicle has no carcols setting yet). Below are **BPM**, **Multiplier** (the time multiplier) and the loop length, a list of shortcuts, and the siren tools with **Solo** and the **Sirens** and **Night** switches. The bottom of the rail stays in view: six numbered colour tiles, tiles for previous step, play or pause, and next step, playback speed, then **Install to resource**, **Save to file…**, **Import…**, **Copy XML**, **Revert**, and on a vehicle **Use my pattern**. The step counter sits over the grid. The grid beside the rail has a column per light head and a row per step. Columns resize with the window so the sirens and lamps fit, down to a narrow minimum; empty, numbered columns fill any space left, and the grid scrolls only when even the narrowest columns do not fit. On shorter windows the rail tightens and the shortcut list folds away.
- **No setting yet.** When the model has siren lights but no setting, the centre offers **New siren setting**, which starts one with a siren per `sirenN` bone, each aimed the way its bone faces, the model's name, and the id carvariations already links when no other setting uses it, and **Import from carcols.meta…**, which starts from a setting in another file.
- **Preview.** A small live view of the model at the top of the right column, with the pattern running and a marker on each siren. By default each light gets a dot and, on a short leader beside it, its number in the siren's colour; heads that sit together share one label, and lights on the far side of the body fade back. Click a number to select that siren (Ctrl-click adds it to the selection). The pin menu over the sequencer's grid switches between these callouts, the **Classic** chips (a dot per siren, numbered when selected) and **Off**. Cortex Studio remembers the style you pick. Drag the preview's bottom edge to make it taller or shorter (double-click for the default). **Expand** (top-right of the preview, or the focus key **\\**) fills the workspace with the preview and its camera controls, with play and the Sirens switch at hand; **Esc** or the same button comes back.
- **Panel.** Under the preview. Its top row is the vehicle's file, with buttons to replace or unload it. Below it, the panel is named for what it shows and follows your selection: it edits the selected sirens. The buttons beside the name switch it to **Setting** (the rest of the setting), **Vehicle** and **Mods** (with a vehicle open) or **Scene**; click the lit button again, or select a siren, to go back. **Vehicle** holds the **Model's own textures**, **Warnings** and **Saved vehicle** rows (a [saved vehicle](/docs/user-guide/workspaces#saved-vehicles) keeps the mods, livery, paint, extras and plate to reopen from Home), the [Equipment](#equipment) list, the switches for the headlights, indicators, brakes, reverse, extra lights and a ULC config's buttons with Glow, Light spill and Fog, and **Resource tools**, which open the [ULC builder](#ulc-builder) and [Rename vehicle](#rename-vehicle). **Mods** switches the vehicle's in-game livery and fits its mod kit parts as in [All](/docs/user-guide/viewer-modes#mod-kits) (lightbars, push bars and antennas often live there), and **Scene** holds the stage, backdrop, lighting and paint slots.

The right column starts wider than the inspector in the other workspaces. Drag its inner edge to resize it, or focus the edge and use the arrow keys; double-click returns it to the default width. The size is remembered. The close button at the end of the panel's buttons, or the right half of the layout icon in the top bar, hides the preview and the panel so the grid gets the room; the button over the grid brings them back. A playing pattern keeps playing while they are hidden.

## The grid

Each siren has a column of 32 steps, first step at the top, drawn in the siren's colour. The number over the column is the `sirenN` bone it drives, and the lamp above it lights in the siren's colour whenever the siren is on, so the row of lamps reads like the lightbar.

- Click a step to switch it on or off; right-click clears it. Drag down a column, or across columns, to paint.
- Paint a step in the next empty column to add a siren there in the colour picked in the rail, and keep dragging down to paint more of its steps. The empty columns after it show how many more sirens the setting can hold.
- Sirens with `rotate` get a second, narrow column for the rotation pattern, marked with a turning arrow.
- The four columns on the right are the head and tail light sequencers (LH, RH, LT, RT) that wig-wag the lamps while sirens run.
- A dimmed column belongs to a siren with flash off. `∅` marks a siren with no light on the model.
- A [dual-colour head](#dual-colour-heads) has one column for its two sirens, numbered like `3+4`.
- **M**, under the number of a selected or hovered column, mutes a siren on the model. **Solo** lights only the selected sirens.

The playing step is outlined across every column, its number is lit in the step column, and the steps firing at that moment brighten under the outline. The counter over the grid shows the step number. Play, pause and step from the rail's tiles, or click a step number to jump there. **Sirens** and **Night** in the rail switch the preview. Undo, redo and the marker menu sit over the grid.

Click a column's number to select it, Ctrl-click to add columns, Shift-click for a range.

## Tools

| Tool | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **Siren** | Adds a siren after the last one, in the opposite colour and phase. |
| **Presets** | Restyles the selected sirens with a set, lowest number first and repeating as needed: red and blue pair, red and white pair, amber strobe, halogen rotator, corner strobes, or a 16-head lightbar. Each siren keeps its aim. With nothing selected, the set is added after the last siren. A pair needs two selected sirens: a siren has one colour, so a pair on one siren gives it only the first. |
| **Dual colour** | Adds a dual-colour head (two sirens, red and blue), pairs two selected sirens into one, or splits a head back into single sirens. See [Dual-colour heads](#dual-colour-heads). |
| Duplicate, move, delete | Copies, reorders or removes the selected sirens. The arrows move the selected heads a column left or right, keeping a dual-colour head together. Moving or deleting changes which bone later sirens drive. |
| **Pattern** | Sets the selected sirens' pattern. The alternating versions give every other selected siren the opposite phase, for wig-wags. **Traffic advisor** builds a pattern across two or more selected sirens, taken in siren number order as one row of heads: an arrow (first to last, last to first, or centre out) that builds over three quarters of a sweep, holds and goes dark; a chase that lights one head at a time; or a warning that alternates the two halves. Each loop of 32 steps holds two sweeps. **Colours take turns** splits the loop between the colours of the selected sirens, so every siren of the first colour flashes together, then every siren of the next (all red, then all blue): steady, or a double, triple or quad flash in each turn. Colours go in the order they first appear, lowest siren number first. It needs two or more colours in the selection. |
| `[` `]` (the up and down arrows), invert | Shifts the pattern one step earlier or later, or swaps lit and dark steps. |
| Copy, paste pattern | Copies the selected siren's pattern onto others. |
| Colours 1 to 6 | Red, blue, amber, white, green and purple lenses. A click, or its number key, picks the colour for sirens you paint into an empty column and colours every selected siren. The picked colour is shown selected. |

Pattern tools edit the flash pattern, or the rotation pattern for a siren that only rotates. A setting holds up to 32 sirens.

## Dual-colour heads

In GTA a siren has one colour, so a light head that flashes red and then blue is two sirens: two `sirenN` bones in the same head, one for each colour, as dual-colour lightbar models have. Sirens shows such a pair as one head with one column:

- Click a step once for the first colour, again for the second, and again for off; right-click clears it. Drag to paint the state you started with. A step where both sirens are on shows both colours side by side.
- Clicking the head selects both sirens, so the panel, the colour swatches and the pattern tools edit them together. **Colours take turns** on a row of dual heads gives every first colour one turn and every second colour the next.
- **Dual colour → Add a dual-colour head** adds a red and blue pair after the last siren. **Pair the selected sirens** joins two selected sirens (a siren already in another head leaves it); **Split into single sirens** gives them their own columns again.

The pair is a view in the editor: carcols.meta stores the two sirens and their patterns as usual, so the file works on any vehicle. carcols.meta has nowhere to keep a pair, so pairs you make on a vehicle are cleared by **Revert**, **Import…**, the file changing on disk while you have no unsaved edits, or closing the tab. The pair lights in game only when the model has a bone for each siren in it; a head with a single bone shows one colour.

## Patterns without a vehicle

With no vehicle open, **New pattern** (in the centre) starts a pattern of four red and blue dual-colour heads. The sequencer, tools, tempo and the panel's siren and setting fields work as they do on a vehicle (lens glow needs a vehicle), and the lamp over each column flashes as the pattern plays. The preview shrinks to **Open a vehicle model**: click it, or drop a vehicle model on it. **Import from carcols.meta…** starts from a setting in any file instead.

**Save to file…** writes the pattern into a carcols.meta you pick (a new file, or an existing one where it is added or replaces the setting with the same id), and **Copy XML** copies its `<Sirens>` Item. The pattern stays in the tab while you open vehicles. On a vehicle, **Use my pattern** (at the bottom of the rail, or in the centre when the vehicle has no setting yet) puts it on sirens 1 and up: colours, patterns, tempo and lamp patterns. The vehicle keeps its id, name, file and each siren's aim, and sirens past the pattern stay as they were. Undo or **Revert** takes it back. The pattern is not kept when the tab or the app closes; the tab asks first while it is unsaved.

## Fields

The panel under the preview edits every carcols field for the selected sirens. When several are selected, a change applies to all of them.

- **Light:** colour (with the alpha carcols stores as `0xAARRGGBB`), intensity, light group, casts light, spot light, cast shadows.
- **Lens glow:** the colour the lit lens glows. **Texture** is the game's look (the lens texture's own colour), **Siren** follows the siren's carcols colour, and **Custom** takes a colour you pick. On its own this changes only the preview, because in game a lens glows in its texture's colour. **Reset all** returns every siren to Texture. The choice is kept with the tab for that model.
- **Write to textures…** (desktop app) puts the lens colours in game. It recolours the parts of the lens textures the tinted sirens light, inside the vehicle's own texture dictionaries (`<vehicle>.ytd` and `<vehicle>+hi.ytd` beside the model). A texture that comes from a pack dictionary in the vehicle's own resource gets a recoloured copy in the vehicle's dictionaries, so other vehicles keep theirs. Textures from the game's files or another resource are never copied: the dialog lists the sirens that use them, and **Texture colour** leaves those lenses in the texture's own colour. Before anything is written, the dialog lists each texture it changes, other lights that use the same part of a texture (they change too), and BC7 textures it stores as DXT5. Sirens that light the same part of a texture must use one colour; the dialog groups them and lets you pick that colour (or the texture's own) in place, then checks again. Textures stored inside the `.yft`, and vehicles without a `.ytd` of their own, can't be written. Each file is first copied to a `.bak` named with the date and time, and the write is all or nothing. Afterwards the lens glow returns to Texture and the model reloads with the new colours; restart the resource in game to load them.
- **Flash:** on or off, **Facing** (eight directions around a top-down vehicle, front at the top, which set the aim), the 32-step pattern as text, multiples, aim (`flashiness` delta), start, sync to bpm, speed and direction. Facing shows the direction the aim matches; an aim between two directions highlights none. As in game, changing the aim also turns the siren's meshes about their bone.
- **Rotation:** on or off, and the same channel fields for the turn.
- **Scale:** on or off and the scale factor the bone grows by while lit.
- **Corona:** intensity, size (0 turns it off), pull and face camera.

**Setting** in the panel holds the name, id, texture, real lights, the light spill falloff and cone angles, light offset, and the head and tail light multiples. The bpm and time multiplier are in the sequencer's rail. Angles stored in radians (`delta`, `start`) are shown in degrees.

## Checks

**Checks** in the rail, under **Status**, counts problems with the setting; click it to list them.

- the id is missing or used by another setting in the resource (these block saving);
- carvariations links a different id, or no siren setting, to the model;
- the bpm is 0, so patterns never advance;
- a siren's pattern has no lit steps, or its multiples are below 1;
- a siren has no bone on the model, or the model has siren bones past the last siren.

## Installing

**Install to resource** writes the setting into the resource in one step:

1. It saves a copy of each file it is about to change beside it, named like `carcols.meta.20261008-143012.bak`.
2. It updates the setting in the resource's own carcols.meta: the file the setting came from, or the first carcols.meta in the resource. Only the values you changed are rewritten, as in [Saving](#saving).
3. It points the model's entry in carvariations.meta at the setting's id. An existing `sirenSettings` gets its value changed; a missing one is added after `lightSettings`. Nothing else in the file changes.

The rail then lists each step. If the model has no carvariations entry the setting is still installed, and the rail says what to add. A step that fails is reported with the steps that did complete. Install is unavailable when the resource has no carcols.meta yet (use **Save to file…** to create one) or while a check blocks saving.

## Importing

**Import…** opens any carcols.meta and reads its siren settings into the editor. When the file holds several, pick one in the rail (or in the centre when there is no setting yet). The import replaces the pattern, sirens and setting fields but keeps the model's id, name and file, so Install and Save still write to the right place. Undo or **Revert** takes it back. On a model with no setting, the import starts a new setting with the id carvariations links (or the imported id when that is free). Importing the vehicle's own setting from its resource's carcols.meta opens that setting for editing instead, so **Install to resource** updates it rather than adding a copy under a new id.

An id above 255 gets a note in **Checks**: singleplayer (LSPDFR) reads such ids only with SirenSetting Limit Adjuster installed.

## Equipment

**Vehicle** in the panel starts with every extra on the model, what it carries (its siren numbers, a steady light, or the ULC button that switches it) and a switch. The list shows which extras the vehicle state turns on and why. A switch overrides that state for the extra until **Reset**. Parts bolt on and drop off the model as you switch them.

## ULC builder

**ULC builder…** writes the vehicle's Ultimate Lighting Controller (ULC) entry in the resource's `ulc.lua`, or creates the file in the resource folder.

- **Vehicles**: the model names the config covers.
- **Buttons**: one row per extra. Switch a row on to give the extra a button, with its label, number key and colour.
- **Park pattern**, **Steady burn**, **Brakes**, **Reverse** and **Horn**: the extras each turns on, and for brakes, reverse and horn the extras each turns off.

It opens on the existing config for the model, or with a button for every extra that carries lights. The right side previews the Lua. **Save** replaces this vehicle's config and keeps fields the builder does not edit (door, sync and speed settings, button flags), adds it to the file when another vehicle's config is there, and backs up the old file first. Comments in an existing `ulc.lua` are not kept in the new file; they stay in the backup. When `fxmanifest.lua` does not mention `ulc.lua`, the result says so. **Copy** copies the table without writing.

## Rename vehicle

**Rename vehicle…** changes the vehicle's spawn name across its resource. Type the new name and the dialog lists every change before anything is written:

- meta files: elements whose whole text is the old name, such as `modelName`, `txdName`, `handlingId`, `gameName`, `handlingName` and texture dictionary relationships;
- Lua scripts: strings that are exactly the old name, such as `ulc.lua` names and `AddTextEntry` keys (`fxmanifest.lua` is never changed, and scripts that are not plain text, such as escrowed files, are skipped and listed);
- stream files: the model (`name.yft`, `name_hi.yft`) and its texture dictionaries (`name.ytd`, `name+hi.ytd`).

Mod kits and mod parts whose names only start with the old name are left alone. Each match keeps its case, so `FPIU25` becomes `PD_FPIU`. Names use letters, digits and underscores, up to 32 characters, and cannot end in `_hi`. Names over 11 characters get a warning.

**Rename** renames the stream files first, all of them or none. Then it saves a `.bak` of each text file and writes the changes. A file that fails is listed, and the rest stay renamed. The model then reopens under its new name. Save or revert siren edits before renaming; the dialog waits until you do.

## Saving

**Save to file…** in the rail asks where to write.

- Pick an existing carcols.meta to update the setting with the same id, or add it to `<Sirens>` when the file has none. Only the values you changed are rewritten, each in the number format the file already uses; added sirens are inserted after the last one and removed sirens are taken out with their comments. Everything else in the file, including comments, spacing, line endings, other settings and tags the editor does not know, stays exactly as it was. A setting whose layout is too unusual to update value by value is rewritten as a whole, in the same place.
- Pick a new file to create a carcols.meta holding only this setting.

Saving the carcols.meta the setting came from replaces it in place, even after you change its id. When a different carcols.meta already holds a setting with the same id, nothing is written until you choose **Replace it** under the save status; to keep both, change this setting's id in **Setting** and save again. If you change the id, update `sirenSettings` in carvariations.meta to match. After a save the workspace reads the meta files again.

**Copy XML** copies the setting's `<Sirens>` Item, so you can paste it into a file yourself.

**Undo** and **Redo** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y) cover every edit. **Revert** in the rail returns to the values in carcols.meta; for a new setting it is **Discard**. Unsaved edits stay on the model until you save or revert, but they are not kept when the tab or the app closes. The **Status** row in the rail opens to a list of what changed against the file, with sirens that changed the same way on one line.

## ELS

Vehicles built for ELS (LSPDFR's ELS V8, ELS-FiveM, ELS-Plus or MISS-ELS) switch their extras from a vehicle configuration file (VCF) instead of the carcols siren setting. Switch **Carcols sirens** to **ELS** (in the rail, or in the centre when the vehicle has no carcols setting) and the rail and the centre column edit the VCF. Cortex Studio remembers the choice for each vehicle, and switching back leaves the carcols setting as it was. Vehicles you never switch work exactly as before.

### Finding the VCF

ELS matches a VCF to the model by name: `police.yft` (or `police_hi.yft`) reads `police.xml`. Cortex Studio looks for that file beside the model, in the folder above it (a FiveM resource or a vehicle download), in subfolders there named `ELS`, `vcf`, `vcfs`, `xmlFiles` or `pack_default`, and then in your **ELS folders**. While a vehicle is still on carcols sirens, the switch in the rail says when it has found a VCF.

- **Add folder…** adds a folder of VCFs, such as `GTA V/ELS/pack_default` (LSPDFR), `resources/ELS-FiveM/vcf` or `resources/MISS-ELS/xmlFiles`. Only a folder that holds a VCF can be added, and Cortex Studio opens that folder only, not its subfolders.
- **Open…** uses any VCF for this vehicle and remembers it.
- **New ELS V8 VCF** and **New MISS-ELS VCF** start one from the model's extras.

### Editing

The bar runs the lights the way the script switches them in game, and a strip shows which ELS extras are lit.

- **ELS V8:** **Stage** Off, 1, 2 or 3. Stage 1 runs the secondary lights. Stage 2 adds the primary extras named by **Stage 2 extras**. Stage 3 runs every primary extra and the warning lights; secondary lights stop at stage 3 when their **Off at stage 3** is on. With all three groups on the CHP format, each stage instead plays one CHP pattern across extras 1 to 10 (the warning pattern at stage 1, secondary at 2, primary at 3), as ELS-FiveM does. The vehicle's carcols siren lights run from the stage set under **Misc**. **Cruise**, **Takedowns** and **Scene** switch those lights.
- **MISS-ELS:** **Primary**, **Secondary** and **Rear reds** play the file's patterns.

The same switches are under **Vehicle** in the panel here and in the Meta tab of Vehicle, where **Lighting** turns ELS on or off for the vehicle. An ELS vehicle's lights run there too, with the edits in this tab.

Under the bar:

- **Lights** (EOVERRIDE) has a row per extra: its role, whether ELS controls it (for MISS-ELS, whether it is listed), its environment light, colour, and the light's offset from the extra. `∅` marks an extra the model does not have. **Add a light** adds one the file does not list.
- **Primary**, **Secondary** and **Warning** (ELS V8) set the lighting format, the pattern each stage starts with (a number or Scan), the stage 2 extras or Off at stage 3, and show a lane per extra with one loop of the pattern and the playing position. The arrows step through the imported patterns in the preview without changing the file; **Preset** goes back to the stage's pattern. **Stage**, **On** and **Off** force a group in the preview.
- **Misc** and **Cruise** (ELS V8) set slicktop, steady burn (extra 10), the stage that also runs the carcols sirens, the arrowboard, takedowns (extra 11), scene lights (extra 12), and the extras cruise mode burns steady.
- **Primary**, **Secondary** and **Rear reds** (MISS-ELS) are grids of flashes, one column per flash, wider for longer flashes. Click or drag across cells to switch lights in a flash. Select a column to set its duration, or to add, duplicate, move or delete a flash. **Statics** lists the lights on their own key, with their names, and shows them in the preview.

With focus in the editor, Space plays or pauses, 0 to 3 pick the ELS V8 stage, and Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y undo and redo.

### Pattern tables

ELS V8 VCFs name patterns by number; the timing behind each number lives in the lighting script, and Cortex Studio does not ship it. **Import from ELS-FiveM…** under **Pattern tables** reads the tables from your own copy: pick the ELS-FiveM resource folder or its `client/_patternTypes` folder (`leds.lua`, `traf.lua`, `chp.lua`). Cortex Studio reads only those three files, as data; nothing in them runs. Without tables, and for a pattern or format the tables lack (`strb`, `rota`, `dro`, `arrw`, `marq`, `custom` or Scan), a group plays a plain alternating flash marked **Approximate**, with the reason. MISS-ELS VCFs carry their own timings, so they always play as written.

### Saving the VCF

The rail lists the VCF's status, format, how many extras ELS controls, the checks, pattern tables and ELS folders, one row each; rows with a list open on click.

Cortex Studio watches the open VCF. When it changes on disk, from a text editor or a Save in another tab, the lights pick up the new values straight away. If you have unsaved edits here, they stay: the status reads **Changed on disk**, and **Load** swaps in the file (discarding the edits) while **Save as…** keeps them. A file that can't be read after a change leaves the last version that loaded playing, and says why. **Live reload** in the rail shows whether the watch is running. With unsaved edits, the **Status** row opens to a list of what they change in the file.

**Save** writes the VCF back after keeping an exact copy beside it, named like `police.xml.20261015-143012.bak`. Only the values you edited change, and only the lines you added or removed come or go; comments, spacing, line endings, the file's text encoding and elements the editor does not know stay as they were. If the file changed on disk after Cortex Studio read it, Save stops instead of overwriting it, and **Save as…** keeps your edits in another file. Save as writes the same VCF, with its sounds, interface and everything else, and starts in the open file's folder, an ELS folder, or beside the model. **Open…** waits until unsaved edits are saved or discarded.

A new VCF saved into ELS-FiveM's `vcf` folder is added to the `vcf_files` list in `vcf.lua` (after a backup), because ELS-FiveM loads only the files listed there. Saved anywhere an ELS script does not read, the rail says where to copy it. **Copy XML** copies the whole VCF. Unsaved VCF edits make the tab ask before it closes, as siren edits do.

### ELS accuracy

ELS switches whole extras, so the preview does too. Lit ELS extras glow at siren brightness, and their environment lights take the VCF colour and offset (read as metres from the extra). Imported tables play at ELS-FiveM's timing, one character every 15 ms. The preview applies the VCF as written; ELS-FiveM itself starts every group at pattern 1 rather than the stage presets, ignores **Off at stage 3** on light groups, and runs the carcols siren lights only at exactly the stage set under **Misc**. Miscs (MISS-ELS mod kit lights) are edited and saved but not shown on the model.

## Cortex Forge

Design and preview lights on the car in Cortex Studio. Check, link and install across your server in Cortex Forge.

**Open in Cortex Studio** on a vehicle in Cortex Forge opens Sirens on that vehicle's carcols.meta with its siren setting selected and its model loaded, in a new tab if Cortex Studio is already running. If the file can't be read, Sirens says so and opens the model alone; if the file has no setting with that id, it lists the settings the file has.

**Check in Forge** opens the resource that holds the open carcols.meta (the nearest folder with an `fxmanifest.lua` or `__resource.lua`) in Cortex Forge, to check it against your server. It sits under **Install to resource**, and in **Rename vehicle…** once the rename is done. It shows only when Cortex Forge 0.1.0 or later is installed, and waits while the setting has unsaved edits, because Forge reads the files on disk.

## Juno

While signed in to **Settings > Cortex account**, the rail gets a **Describe the lights** field that writes flash patterns from a sentence about where the lights sit on the car. See [Juno in Sirens](/docs/user-guide/juno).

## Accuracy

The preview follows the game for timing, step order, colours, `scale` growth and which geometry each siren drives. As in game, a siren's colour tints its corona and the light it casts; its lit mesh glows in the colour its texture gives it, unless you pick another **Lens glow** to preview or write into the texture. Rotation speed, multiples, unsynced speed and brightness are close approximations; see [Meta Simulation](/docs/user-guide/meta-simulation#lights). The editor writes values exactly as entered.

Sirens needs the desktop app: it reads and writes the resource's meta files, `ulc.lua`, VCFs and stream files. In the browser, ELS can still start a new VCF and copy it as XML.
